STEP 1 -- CALIBRATION AGAINST THE KNOWN ANSWER (stock vs stock, n=80)
  A wins 37/80 = 0.463, exact 95% CI [0.350, 0.578]; one-sided P(X>=37)=0.783.
    -> consistent with 0.500. The harness is not biased toward either arm.
  Draws: 0/80.
  Skipped frames: 0 across all 80 episodes (server "Frame pacing" line), so
    no bot ever lost a decision to CPU contention at this parallelism.
  Side split, same binary both sides: red 47/80 = 0.588, 95% CI [0.472,0.696];
    A scored 0.550 seated red and 0.375 seated blue -- a 17.5pp swing between
    arms of the SAME binary. The historical red bias (28-12, p=0.0166) is
    still present in direction. Alternation is mandatory; a pinned side is not
    interpretable.

STEP 2 -- CRITERION (derived from step 1's measured variance)
  N = 160 decisive episodes, 80 seatings per side. Draws are excluded from
  the denominator and reported separately (0/80 in calibration).
  Step 1 establishes the outcome is binomial with p ~ 0.5, so SE = 0.5/sqrt(N).
  N=160 gives SE = 3.95pp and 85% power against a true 0.60 at one-sided
  alpha=0.05. 0.60 is the smallest win rate that would justify a generation of
  work on a learned controller; at the calibration's own n=80 the same test
  has only 57% power, which is why the head-to-head is doubled.

  "THE HEADROOM IS REAL" requires BOTH:
    C1  exact one-sided binomial vs p=0.5 at alpha=0.05
          -> k >= 91 of 160 (0.5687); P(X>=91)=0.0483.
    C2  win rate >= 0.578, the exact upper 95% bound of the A/A calibration's
        OWN win rate. A rate the reference case could itself have produced is
        not headroom.
          -> k >= 93 of 160 (0.5813).
  Binding threshold: 93 wins of 160.

  VALIDATION OF THE CRITERION AGAINST THE REFERENCE (lessons #10):
  applying the identical rule to the calibration battery requires 48/80 (C1)
  and 47/80 (C2); the reference scored 37/80. The reference FAILS the bar,
  by 10 wins, and its 95% upper bound sits at C2 rather than above it. The
  bar is not one the reference case would pass.

  Anything below 93 is reported as "not met". A result at or below 0.5 is
  reported as a negative result and the heuristic is NOT retuned to chase a
  win -- the first honest attempt is the answer.

SECONDARY METRICS (reported with 95% intervals, not gating)
  captures/ep, kills/ep, teamkills/ep, deaths/ep, per arm and paired A-B.

VARIANT UNDER TEST (-d:dynRole), one rule, both directions
  Recomputed every frame inside decide(), from `ownStolen` -- our own pedestal
  is never fogged, so it is the ONE piece of team state all eight seats read
  identically on the same frame:
    heart on its pedestal -> the HomeDefender seat is guarding nothing:
                             it plays MidBottom (7 attackers, 1 sniper).
    heart off its pedestal -> Overwatch and MidGuard drop to HomeDefender
                             (3 guards) until the heart is home.
  Overwatch is only ever removed, never created: its post is scanned once at
  nav-build, so a seat promoted into it mid-game would hold a post that was
  never picked for it.

SIDE VARIANCE, measured not assumed (calibration split 0.550 red / 0.375 blue)
  Balanced alternation is a BLOCKED design (80 episodes per side stratum), so
  the side effect is removed from the variance, not added to it:
    unstratified Var 1.5537e-3 (SE 3.94pp)  <- the interval reported
    stratified   Var 1.5059e-3 (SE 3.88pp)  <- the design's true variance
    side component 4.785e-5 = 3.1% of total; reported CI is 1.6% too wide.
  So a middle-band result is ordinary binomial noise at N=160, and the cheap
  resolution is more episodes rather than a better heuristic.

======================= CORRECTION, 2026-08-31 =======================
RETRACTED from the result section above: "in the 87 capture-free episodes the
winner also out-killed the loser in 85 (97.7%)" as EVIDENCE that attrition
decides games. It is an accounting identity, not a finding.

Checked across all 141 capture-free decisive episodes in both batteries:
  loser team deaths      = 24 in 141/141   (3 lives x 8 seats: a full wipe)
  winner team deaths     = 13..23
  loser_deaths - (winner_kills + loser_teamkills) = 0 in 141/141
Losing by elimination IS having 24 deaths, and every death is attributed to a
killer with no hazard deaths, so the winner's kill count is 24 minus the
loser's own teamkills BY CONSTRUCTION. The 97.7% measured the definition of
elimination. The two exceptions are episodes where the WINNER's own teamkills
were large enough to flip the raw comparison. This is docs/lessons.md #3: a
proxy definitionally tied to the outcome it is used to explain.

WHAT SURVIVES, and it is stronger (n=239 decisive, both batteries pooled):
  capture-ending      98 (41.0%)   loser deaths mean 22.3/24, median 23, min 15
  elimination-ending 141 (59.0%)   loser deaths 24/24 by definition
  captures landing with the loser already at >= 22/24 deaths: 83/98 = 84.7%
                                              >= 23/24 deaths: 60/98 = 61.2%
  episodes where the loser ended below 15/24 deaths: 0/239
  winner deaths are the SAME in both modes (19.7 capture / 19.2 elimination),
  so capture episodes are not "the winner cruised".

  Not one decisive episode was resolved before the loser had spent >= 62% of
  its life pool. The capture is not an alternative win condition reached
  early; it is the finisher on an attrition race that is ~93% complete. So
  "captures rarely fire" and "attrition decides games" are the SAME fact.

  This is a statement about how games TERMINATE, which is observed, not about
  a correlation between two derived statistics, which was the retracted claim.

WHAT I STILL CANNOT SHOW: that an EARLY kill lead causes the win. results.json
carries end-of-episode team totals only. For elimination endings the question
is close to ill-posed (elimination is 24 kills against you). The answerable
version -- does a kill lead at tick T predict the win, controlling for seats
alive -- needs tick-stamped events, which baseline/artlog.nim's events.jsonl
carries. One 40-episode battery with --artlog (~15 min) would answer it.

INDEPENDENT REPLICATION on the maps the league actually runs (mapPath "gen",
3211x1713, vs this battery's fixed 1235x659 arena), from
paintbot-rl/docs/play-analysis-rl11.md, 100 champion self-play episodes
measured by a different agent with different code:
  champion self-play  kills 21.4  deaths 21.6/24  captures 0.11 per team-game
  Mean deaths of 21.6 of 24 reproduces the near-exhaustion picture exactly
  (this battery: loser 24, winner 19.2, mean 21.6). The finding is not an
  artifact of the arena map.

WHAT THAT MEANS, plainly
  On the build the league runs, the capture is still the finisher on an
  attrition race: every one of 160 games was resolved after the loser had
  spent >= 17 of 24 lives (71%), the median capture lands with one life
  left on the losing side, and the mean capture-ending game has the loser
  at 91.5% of its pool (baseline 93%). The capture share is identical to the
  baseline's (41.9% vs 41.0%). The strategic conclusion -- kill exchange
  rate is the real objective -- stands on the champion.

  What is WEAKER on the champion, and should be said with the claim:
  captures land a little earlier in the race. The >= 23 share drops from
  61% to 45% (p = 0.04), the >= 22 share from 85% to 73% (p = 0.08), and
  the earliest capture lands at 17/24 rather than 15/24 on a smaller
  sample -- so the "~93% complete" figure is 91.5% here, and 1 in 4
  captures now lands with the loser holding 3+ lives (vs 1 in 6.5). Read
  "finisher" as "the last 1-3 kills by another name", not "the last kill".

  The interrupted arm's "different game" (2.5x frames, 0.75 captures/ep)
  does not reproduce on the live champion: frames are 2.27x, captures/ep
  0.42. That arm was the REPO baseline source at the 47-define v25 set,
  which includes -d:gv36SlotHunt -- the define v26 dropped because it broke
  the bot's firing under the GV40 engine. It is not the champion, and its
  18 episodes should not be cited for anything.
