Precomputed Shared Cog Navigation

Current memory ruling (2026-09-09). James authorized up to a fourfold increase in non-colossal memory caps. The research harness now uses a 32 MiB shared-navigation limit on non-colossal maps (twice the previous 16 MiB), with the existing 256 MiB total-navigation limit unchanged, including colossal. The integrated C2 shared-visibility cache has a largest measured non-colossal shared footprint of 32,464,268 bytes; fresh capacity-ledger checks pass all 76 measured maps (including allocation allowances, not an RSS guarantee); timing and activation-time qualification remain incomplete. Earlier measurements and limits below describe historical designs. Timing, quality, determinism and activation requirements are unchanged. See the ruling and measured rationale.

This document describes a rework of route planning in the Season 2 cog body. The rework replaces the per-seat, budgeted, map-sized A* with four parts: one immutable room/portal route index per map, one zone hazard overlay per episode, synchronous bounded route queries, and deterministic local steering. The design below records the original proposal at fad3029f. The current research branch implements resumable mixed-grid queries with one shared pop-budgeted workspace; its implementation and open qualification gates are tracked in the research scoreboard. Claude drafted the document for James. A Codex reviewer challenged it in three rounds, and its fact checks are included (see §10). Reference PRs #408, #409 and #410 are inputs to this design. Do not merge them.

Executive Summary

Season 2 cogs stand still when they must walk. A cog with no route gets its own position as a waypoint, and thus it emits an empty movement mask. A cog that completed an older route and then gets a new goal keeps the completed route loaded. The engine reports this cog as "walking an older route", but the cog does not move. Four mechanisms cause this. Every standing order cancels the pending plan. The planner is a resumable A* over a 4 px lattice of up to 344,487 nodes, rationed at 256 work units per seat per tick. The scheduler gives no priority to seats with no route. Dead seats continue to spend budget. On giant pool maps, cold far routes take 10-21 scheduler ticks. When goals alternate for 32 ticks, the planner spends and discards every permitted unit, and 260-483 of 512 seat-ticks give no movement. Each seat also holds about 68 MB of planner workspace sized to the full lattice, which is about 2.2 GB for 32 seats.

The proposal keeps BodyMap, the exact collision predicate, the cost model, the follower shape and the per-seat danger field. It replaces the planner with a hierarchical route index built once per map install. The rooms and chokes from BodyMap become a graph of portal sides. Precomputed intra-room segments and per-portal crossings join the sides, and next-hop portal fields cover the inside of each room. A bounded A* over that side graph, plus bounded endpoint refinement, answers every route request in the same call, in integer fixed-point arithmetic. The query validates the route descriptor in shared scratch and then copies it atomically into about 12 KB of per-seat state. A zone hazard overlay prices paint arrival as a finite cost, never as a prune. An eight-octant steering rule moves the cog when no advancing route exists. The probe measured the shared index at about 3.5 MB on giant maps and about 16.3 MiB on colossal. The index build took 189-283 ms against a 479-499 ms BodyMap baseline. The gates of the task decide every estimate in this document: route cost inflation p95 ≤ 3 %, body slice p95 ≤ 4 ms, activation ≤ 2x baseline. The main accepted trade is that a static segment cannot avoid danger inside a room.

1 · Problem

  • Two verified player-visible stalls: a life with no route yet, and a finished route that survives a new goal.
  • Four mechanisms compound them: cancel-on-intent, a rationed lattice A*, a scheduler with no priority for route-less seats, and dead seats that keep planning.
  • Planner workspace is sized to the full lattice for every seat: ~68 MB per seat on a giant map, ~2.2 GB at 32 seats.
  • The focused test suites are green because the liveness cases are uncovered, not because they pass.

The tick-by-tick body report1 found the symptom: Season 2 cogs stand still when they must walk. Players can see two cases. The code shows both.

The first case is a cog whose current life has no route. When the path length is zero, the waypoint function of the follower returns the position of the cog2. As a result, the seat tick emits an empty movement mask and records the diagnostic state bnsNoPath3. The second case is a cog that reached the endpoint of an older route and then gets a new goal. The completed route stays loaded, and its cursor stays on the last point. That point is the position of the cog. The engine reports the state bnsStalePath, "walking an older route"4, but the movement mask is zero.

Four mechanisms make it worse

Order churn discards almost complete work. setStandingIntent cancels the pending plan on every new standing order5. That cancel occurs before the navigation waypoint code applies its two-cell goal hysteresis6. Thus the hysteresis never protects a plan in flight.

The planner rations its work across ticks. The planner is a resumable weighted A* on a 4 px lattice7. A pooled budget of 256 work units per configured seat per tick drives it8. On a giant pool map the lattice has 344,487 nodes. The task measured that a far route needs about 137k work units9. Under the share of one seat, that is hundreds of ticks.

The scheduler has no priority. The round-robin over seats does not prefer seats that have no usable route10.

Dead seats continue to plan. Death only changes the active flag of the seat11. The planning scheduler never reads that flag12. Thus a dead seat can continue to spend work units that live seats need.

Measurements

The task measurements9 come from an arm64 release build with 16 seats on battle-royale pool maps 0, 21, 42 and 63. Cold far routes complete in 10-21 scheduler ticks. When goals alternate for 32 ticks, the planner spends and discards all 131,072 permitted work units on every map. The zero-movement counts are 260/512, 286/512, 483/512 and 428/512 seat-ticks. A no-path seat at index 15 waited 6-11 ticks for its first planning visit. The peak process RSS was about 2.37 GB. The focused suites pass (test_shell_body_nav 20/20, test_shell_body_seat 22/22). Thus the liveness cases are not covered. They are not red.

2 · What the design keeps from the existing code

  • BodyMap stays the immutable per-episode terrain layer, including rooms, chokes and goal validation.
  • The exact collision predicate, the cost model, the follower shape and the per-seat danger field are all retained unchanged.
  • The zone paint-arrival field and three reference PRs supply the hazard projection, the safety hints and the semantic target contract.

BodyMap. BodyMap is already an immutable per-episode terrain layer. It holds 1 px wall and clearance rasters (a chessboard distance transform), pixel components, and the 8 px nav grid. On that grid, NavCell = 8, and a cell is walkable when the clearance at its center is more than PlayerHalf (6 px). It computes watershed rooms over clearance. Chokes sit at room contacts, and buildTopology removes chokes closer than 64 px (GateSeparationPx)16. It also holds the cover atlas, the validator tables, and the home fields. validateGoal is the only constructor of ValidatedGoal17. One convention is important for the index build. roomLabelAt returns a one-based label, but BodyRoom and the roomA/roomB fields of BodyChoke are zero-based indices18.

The exact collision predicate. A move is legal when canStand and the pixel-exact segmentClear both pass. The current planner tests both for every lattice step19.

The cost model. The cost of a step is its length x (1 + profileDangerWeight x danger at the midpoint of the step). When the nav cell of the midpoint is the blocked-penalty cell, the cost is multiplied by 8 (FollowBlockFactor). The profile danger weights are cpDefault 1.0, cpCarrier 2.5 and cpHunter 0.2520.

The follower shape. The follower has a path buffer with a cursor. The waypoint advances when the cog is within one NavCell. The corridor is 20 px. The cog is stuck after 8 ticks. The blocked penalty lasts 96 ticks21.

The per-seat danger field and its rebuild, which occurs every 32 ticks with a stagger and uses at most eight visible enemies22.

The zone paint-arrival field. The sim builds one ZoneArrivalField per episode. The field has two surfaces. The damage surface is not gated, and zoneDamageByPaint reads it. The arrival surface is gated by the render layer, and it can say "never" under wall art. zonePaintedForDamageAt compares damage with the elapsed clock of the schedule (sim.tickCount - sim.gameStartTick). The grid stride is 4 px (ZoneFieldCellPx). The sentinel is ZoneNeverArrives = 0xFFFF. ensureZoneArrivalField builds the field on first use23.

From the reference PRs. PR #408 gives four items: the conservative MIN projection of the 4 px field onto the 8 px nav grid, the finite hazard price (never a prune), the derated ETA speed of MaxSpeed x 3 / (MotionScale x 4) = 2.0625 px/tick, and the 48-tick safe-horizon bucket24. The top speed is 704/256 = 2.75 px/tick25. PR #409 gives the fog-safe integer nav hints: ticks until paint here, ticks to safety, geodesic safe distance in px, and a retreat octant26. PR #410 gives navigate_to with target_class = "zone_safe_ground" and a required literal fallback point, which the engine resolves27.

  • Giant pool maps have 85,814 nav cells in 43-107 rooms with 212-369 chokes. Colossal has 343,256 cells, 302 rooms and 1,237 chokes.

4 · Proposed design

  • One immutable route index per map install, shared by every seat. One immutable hazard overlay per episode. A small bounded safe cache per horizon bucket.
  • Every route request is answered synchronously in the same call by a bounded A* over the portal-side graph plus bounded endpoint refinement.
  • The follower reads the legs from the index. The endpoint legs are copied into small fixed per-seat buffers. A deterministic eight-octant rule steers when no advancing route exists.
  • All route choice is integer arithmetic with no clocks, threads, pointer identity or hash-iteration order.

In one sentence: build one immutable room/portal route index per map install, attach one immutable zone hazard overlay per episode, answer every route request in the same call with a bounded search over the portal-side graph plus bounded endpoint refinement, execute the selected legs from the index, and steer locally with a deterministic eight-octant rule when no advancing route exists.

4.1 BodyRouteIndex (map install, immutable, episode-shared)

The build makes the index from BodyMap inside the same activation barrier, before newBodyNavSystem. The index is deterministic by construction. Every loop runs in room index, choke index and row-major cell order. There are no tables keyed by pointer and no hash iteration.

The index holds six kinds of data. The sizes are for pool:1 (giant) and colossal.

  1. Legal moves. legalMoves: seq[uint8] holds one byte per nav cell. Bit i is set when the move to the canonical Neighbors[i] is legal: both cells are walkable, the move does not cut a corner, and pixel segmentClear passes between the centers. This is the one legality oracle for the index build, the smoothing of the follower, the local search and the steering. segmentClear and Neighbors move into the body map module, so that there is exactly one implementation. Today the map and the planner each have a copy31. Size: 86 KB giant / 343 KB colossal.
  2. Room compaction. roomOf: seq[uint16] holds the zero-based room index per nav cell. roomCellStart and roomCells hold the cell indices per room in row-major order. localIndex: seq[int32] holds the index of each nav cell inside its room. The build asserts that every walkable cell has a room. The probe found that this is always true on 8 maps (§3). Size: about 0.6 MB / 2.4 MB.
  3. Portals. Portals are the BodyMap chokes, with two sides. For each choke, the build stores an anchor cell on the roomA side and one on the roomB side. Each anchor is the nearest walkable cell of that room to the choke pixel, found within a bounded ring in a fixed ring order. The build also stores the legal crossing between the two anchors. The crossing is a direct legal edge, or a bounded build-time search whose cells become a crossing segment. Activation asserts that every portal reaches both rooms. The one-sided rule of the probe gave 0-12 anchor mismatches per map (§3).
  4. Portal fields. For each (room r, portal p of r), the index holds one uint8 next-hop per cell of r. The next-hop is the index of the legal neighbor toward the anchor of p on the side of r. One room-restricted Dijkstra computes it, and ties go to Neighbors order. The index does not store distances. A query that needs the distance from a cell to p walks the chain. Exact scoring walks the chain in any case. The measured maximum chain is 213 cells on giant and 436 on colossal. The Dijkstra uses one transient uint32 distance array over one room, and the build reuses it. Size: 1.7 MB / 9.5 MB.
  5. Intra-room segments. For every portal pair (p < q) of a room, the index holds the cell path p → q, read from the Dijkstra parents of p. The path is flat (segmentCells: seq[int32] with start and length), with the static length as uint32 in 1/16 px and a bounding box. Pairs that are not reachable inside the room get no segment (measured 1-5 % of pairs). The abstract graph routes around them through other rooms. Size: 0.9 MB / 3.1 MB.
  6. Portal-side graph. The nodes are the (portal, incident room) sides, at most 2,474 on colossal. The edges are the intra-room segments between sides of the same room (at most 27,460 directed) plus one crossing edge per portal between its two sides (at most 2,474 directed). Because the crossing is an edge, a room change always pays the length, danger, blocked and hazard cost of the crossing. Every selected edge names exactly one segment.

Budget. The retained total is about 3.5 MB on giant (limit 16 MiB) and about 16.3 MiB on colossal (limit 32 MiB). The transient build workspace is one heap and one distance array over one room (at most 70,041 cells), and the build reuses it. The build time of the probe is an upper bound for this shape. It was 189-283 ms on giant maps against a 479-499 ms BodyMap baseline (total 1.4-1.6x the baseline, cap 2x). It was 2,245 ms on colossal against 2,263 ms (total 2.0x, cap 3x)29. The canonical linux/amd64 numbers come from the gate run of subtask 02.

Coverage proof. Rollout subtask 02 runs an offline, exhaustive proof on every supported map. Every standable pixel must have a legal pixel connector of at most 32 px to an anchored nav cell of the same pixel component. Two anchored cells must be connected in the graph exactly when their pixel components are equal. The probes already show that pool:0 splits one pixel component into two legal 8 px components (a 34-pixel pocket). Thus this is an invariant to prove, not a sample to hope for. If the proof fails on a map, the build adds sparse 4 px micro-corridor nodes where the proof fails, before qualification. The route corpus is the cost test only. It is never the topology proof.

4.2 BodyHazardOverlay (episode, immutable) and BodySafeCache (episode, mutable, bounded)

Server seam. The seam is in resetFirstLightForSim32, after newBodyMap. When season2Shell and zoneDamageByPaint and zonePhases.len > 0, the server calls ensureZoneArrivalField(sim), takes a snapshot of the damage surface (never arrival), the grid dimensions and the field fingerprint, builds the overlay, and then constructs the nav system. In all other cases the overlay is a typed dark state: there is no hazard term, the hints are absent, and class targets cannot resolve, so the literal fallback of the play stands. Nothing is built on first use on the play path.

The overlay is relative to the schedule and has no origin. gameStartTick is -1 until startGame enters Playing33, and that occurs after the construction of FirstLight. Thus the overlay takes no snapshot of an origin. The server passes the live elapsed zone tick (the same sim.tickCount - sim.gameStartTick that every zone consumer reads) into the FirstLight step. The hazard ETA, the safe buckets and the hints all use that tick.

Overlay data. arrival: seq[uint16] holds, per nav cell, the MIN over the 4 px source cells that the nav cell covers (the conservative projection of PR #40824). Per segment, arrivalMin/arrivalMax let the query skip segments that are provably zero-risk or provably saturated. Per room, maxDamageArrival treats never-arrives as infinity. Size: 172 KB giant / 686 KB colossal.

Safe cache. The key is (overlay fingerprint, game generation, horizon bucket of 48 ticks). The cache is recomputed once per bucket roll, and it is re-keyed if the elapsed time moves backwards. The set of dry portal sides (arrival later than the bucket end plus the horizon) seeds one multi-source Dijkstra over the side graph with static lengths. The result is safeDistPx[side] and safeNext[side]. The per-room flag "contains a dry cell" comes from maxDamageArrival in O(rooms), which is 302 on colossal. This is microseconds of work. No full-grid flow field and no full-grid scan runs on a bucket roll. (PR #408 built a full field again every 48 ticks. That is rejected.)

Per-seat safety answers obey the contract of PR #40926. The answers are integers, and they are fog-safe because the paint front is public. If the own cell of the cog is dry, ticks_to_safety is 0. If not, and the own room has dry cells, a bounded legal BFS from the cog (fixed cap, 512 cells) finds the nearest dry cell. In all other cases the answer is the best of (walked chain distance to side s + safeDistPx[s]) over the portal sides of the room. The direction is the first step of the winner, as an octant. Every resolved source must satisfy the dry predicate (asserted). If not, the answer is "unreachable", and the literal point of the play stands. The identity of a zone_safe_ground target is (class, resolved source cell). The bucket is cache provenance, not intent identity, so an unchanged target never causes route churn. Resolution runs before the arrived test in seatTick, so the effective goal is the class target, not the fallback.

4.3 Live route query (synchronous, bounded, runs only when a request changes)

The inputs are the seat position, the ValidatedGoal, the cost profile, the danger field of the seat, the blocked-penalty cell, the overlay, and the elapsed zone tick.

  1. Endpoint attach, both ends. The candidate anchor cells are within a ring of at most 4 nav cells (32 px, the current EndpointSnapPx34), in fixed ring order. A candidate is accepted only if the exact pixel segment between the anchor and the endpoint passes segmentClear. No candidate is a typed failure. Then the follower keeps an advancing old route if it has one, or it steers. The route always ends at goal.goalPoint itself, as the path of the current planner does35. The play path does not use BodyMap.nearestWalkable, whose ring has no bound36.
  2. Same-room candidates apply when the start and the goal share a room. Candidate (a) is the straight line, if every cell step along it is legal. Candidate (b) goes through the best portal side of the room. The query walks both next-hop chains (O(degree x chain) reads) and stays inside the room. Candidate (c) is a bounded weighted room-local A* over legalMoves in a shared generation-stamped workspace, with a fixed expansion cap. The cap is a constant derived from latency. 4,096 is the first hypothesis. The cap is never derived from room size, because rooms reach 70,041 cells. The query scores all candidates with the full cost model, and the cheapest wins. A cap hit only removes candidate (c).
  3. Side-graph A*. The start and the goal are virtual nodes. Exactly scored chains (danger, blocked cell, hazard advanced cell by cell) attach them to the portal sides of their rooms. The edge cost = static length + danger-weighted cost + blocked factor + hazard price. The heuristic is the exact integer octile distance, 181 x min(dx, dy) + 128 x (max(dx, dy) - min(dx, dy)), over cell-index deltas between anchors. That heuristic is admissible for the encoded metric. The golden oracle states this formula and every rounding rule, so the integer implementation and the reference cannot disagree by convention. Ties go to (cost, side index). A constant expansion cap of 4,096 pops, more than the side count of any supported map, only guards against bugs. The workspace arrays have the size of the side count, and all seats share them (queries are sequential). They have generation stamps, so the query clears nothing.
  4. Segment-cost memo. One shared, query-local memo keyed by edge ID holds the danger-weighted segment costs. Each directed edge has a 4 B value plus a 2 B generation stamp (27,460 x 6 B = 165 KB on colossal, counted in the retained shared total). Nothing is memoised per seat, and nothing is cleared per query. The blocked factor applies to segments whose bounding box contains the blocked cell and whose cell list contains it.
  5. Hazard price per edge. The query starts from the entry ETA. It advances the ETA along the cells of the segment by their 1/16 px lengths at 2.0625 px per tick (an integer ratio), and applies the finite ramp at each cell in traversal order. The per-segment extrema only skip segments that are provably zero or provably saturated. The price is finite. It is never a prune.
  6. Assemble and install. The query assembles the whole route descriptor in shared query scratch: the ordered edge IDs with direction, the copied start leg or local leg, the copied goal leg, and the exact endpoint connectors. It validates every leg. Only then does it copy the descriptor into the fixed buffers of the seat and change the route revision. The edge-descriptor buffer holds 4,096 entries. That bound is safe because a non-negative shortest path is simple, and activation asserts it. The start/local and goal leg buffers hold 512 cells each. Their size comes from the search cap and the measured maximum chain (213 cells giant, 436 colossal). A failed query never touches the active route.

Cost estimate on giant, to be replaced by the numbers of subtask 09: the attach step is at most 2 x 46 sides x at most 213 cells, about 20k cell reads. The A* is at most 738 sides and 8,748 directed edges with O(1) memoised edge cost. The segments that the search relaxes bound a cold memo fill. In the worst case that is every segment, 224,822 cell reads, about 0.2-0.4 ms. This is the one estimate that can put the 5 ms maximum at risk, if all 16 seats query cold in the same tick. Thus the benchmark includes exactly that burst, and the fallback is to sample every second cell.

4.4 Following and immediate movement (every navigate tick)

  • Legs, not a map-sized path. The follower executes the legs of the descriptor. The start/local leg and the goal leg come from the copied buffers of the seat. The middle legs come from the index by (edge id, direction, offset). The crossings come from the stored crossing cells. The last leg is the pixel connector to the exact goal. The waypoint advance, corridor, stuck and blocked-penalty rules keep their current values.
  • Follow-time smoothing. The follower looks ahead up to K = 6 cells along the current leg. It targets the farthest point whose exact pixel segment from the actual position of the cog passes segmentClear. This removes portal-point kinks and octile zig-zags, and it is what keeps short gate crossings under the 3 % gate. The corpus measures executed masks, so the smoothing is inside the measurement.
  • Advancing route. A route advances when its next waypoint is farther than the arrive radius from the cog. An arrived endpoint is not an advancing route.
  • Local steering runs when there is no advancing route (a failed query, or an exhausted route while the goal changed). It evaluates the eight octants in Neighbors order. The candidate is self + 8 px along the octant. It rejects the candidate if canStand fails, if pixel segmentClear fails, or if the candidate is the blocked-penalty cell. Score = distance-to-target reduction (target = the next waypoint if any, or the goal) minus profile-weighted danger minus hazard price, in integers. It selects the maximum, and ties go to octant order. If no candidate makes progress, the cog stands and records bnsNoProgress. The rule is O(8) and allocates nothing.
  • Diagnostic states become: idle, following, steering, no-progress, arrived. bnsStalePath is removed. It can no longer claim motion while the cog is stationary.

4.5 Request lifecycle

  • setStandingIntent no longer touches navigation. The cancel5 and the goal pin both go, and the pins go with the route-field slots. The nav coordinator owns every request decision.
  • Route anchor = the goal cell of the last accepted request, installed or failed. A query runs if and only if one of these conditions is true: there is no route in this life, the goal cell differs from the anchor by more than 2 cells (ReplanGoalCells) on an axis, the profile changed, the seat was stuck for 8 ticks, the goal moves and 12 ticks passed since the last query, or the current route is exhausted. Sub-threshold moves do not move the anchor, so cumulative one-cell drift causes a query in the end. Epoch and provenance changes are invisible to navigation.
  • Queries are synchronous. Thus there is no pending state and nothing to cancel, and the replacement is ready in the same call. After a failed query, the seat keeps the old route only while it advances. If not, the seat steers.
  • Death (resetAfterDeath11) clears the route descriptors, the leg buffers, the cursor, the anchor, the revision, lastXy, stuckTicks, the blocked penalty, the last-query tick, the follow counters, the desired profile and moving flags, and the semantic target identity. Then it calls setSeatActive(false). The danger rebuild already skips inactive seats37, and an assertion makes sure that no query touches an inactive seat. The shared index, the overlay and the safe cache persist.

4.6 Arithmetic and determinism

Search costs are integers in 1/16 px. An orthogonal step is 128, and a diagonal step is 181, with the exact integer octile heuristic from §4.3. A Euclidean heuristic in these units is not admissible: 181/128 = 1.4140625 < √2, and 53 diagonal steps already show the gap. The search samples danger from the existing float32 field and quantises it to 1/256 at the point of use. That is at most 0.49 % relative error on a carrier-profile path, and the report compares it with the unquantised oracle. Profile weights are integer ratios over 256 (256, 640, 64). The blocked factor 8 is exact. The hazard ramp is in integer ticks. Every rational product rounds to nearest. Totals are in checked int64. The cost-gate oracle stays the 4 px float formula of the task, and it scores the returned route. The gate must see the quantisation of the search, so the oracle never adopts it.

Route choice uses no clocks, threads, async work, pointer identity or hash-iteration order. Replays re-drive input masks, and no body_* module is in the wasm build (PR #408 showed this24). Thus archived replays stay valid. Movement masks do change. Thus a collision-free GameVersion (currently "52"38), all nine replay fixtures39 and the committed replay viewer are recorded again after the gates pass.

4.7 Deleted

The rework deletes BodyPlanner and its lattice workspace, BodyFieldMinter, the route-field slots in BodySeatCache (MaxRouteFieldsPerSeat, pins, peekRouteDistance), the plan and mint jobs, ColdPlanBudgetPerTick, runPlanningTick, prewarmColdPlans, the plan budget events and traces, the scheduler cursors, and bnsStalePath. The duck cache stays, because cover-hold owns it. Per-seat route-query state decreases from about 68 MB to about 12 KB (edge descriptors 8 KB, two 512-cell leg buffers 4 KB). The per-seat danger and visited rasters (0.7 MB giant, 2.75 MB colossal) do not change, and they are outside this task. A uint8 danger raster with a shared visited raster is a listed follow-up.

5 · Decisions and rejected alternatives

  • A hierarchical room/portal index over BodyMap's existing rooms and chokes, with 8 px cells and exact pixel endpoint connectors.
  • One static segment per portal pair, scored dynamically. A portal-side graph, so that crossings are always paid. Integer fixed-point costs.
  • Each record below names the decision, what lost, and why it lost.
Decision

An HPA*-style room/portal index over BodyMap's rooms and chokes.

Rejected: JPS, because its benefit assumes uniform static costs, and here the costs are profile-weighted danger, blocked cells and zone time. Recast/Detour, which has the correct behavior model, but it is a C++ 3D navmesh and an FFI dependency for a deterministic integer-bitmap Nim sim. D* Lite, which targets edge costs that change, but here the topology is static and the goals churn. Continuum crowds, which amortise shared destinations, but the seats have distinct goals and danger fields. No maintained Nim package covers deterministic hierarchical game navigation. Also rejected: the resumable scheduler with priority for no-route seats. It still needs map-sized per-seat state, it still takes hundreds of ticks for far routes, and steering alone lets cogs walk greedily into dead ends for seconds.

Decision

8 px cells with centre anchors and exact pixel endpoint connectors as the baseline.

Rejected as baseline: a 4 px index, which needs 4x the field memory (about 76 MB on colossal, more than the limit). Per-cell sub-lattice anchors plus sparse 4 px micro-corridor nodes, which the Codex reviewer preferred. That option stays as the documented contingency if the corpus gate reports a missing route. The probes show that the spawn component keeps 98.7-99.3 % of standable pixels, and the connector covers the rest on the sampled maps.

Decision

One static segment per portal pair, scored dynamically, plus bounded weighted refinement in the endpoint rooms. This is the explicit trade of the task: "dynamic global danger may select among precomputed structural route segments; the engine does not need the exact cell-by-cell dynamic optimum."

Rejected: k alternative segments per pair, which costs more memory with no evidence that it is necessary. The gate decides.

Decision

One shared, query-local segment-cost memo keyed by edge ID.

Rejected: an eager per-seat refresh at every danger rebuild, which touches every segment every 32 ticks whether or not a query uses it (13,730 segments per seat per generation on colossal). A per-seat lazy memo, which is 13,730 x 4 B = 55 KB per seat on colossal, against the intent that per-seat query state stays small.

Decision

A route is ordered edge IDs with direction over a portal-side graph, with legs executed from the index, endpoint legs copied into small fixed buffers, and the whole descriptor validated in shared scratch before an atomic copy.

Rejected: a per-seat flat cell buffer with prefix truncation, which adds a second query/install lifecycle for a case that "cannot occur". A prefix that is accepted in silence is not a route. An ordered portal list over collapsed portal nodes, which lets the search change rooms without payment for the crossing, and which cannot say which of two parallel segments won. Live descent on the static portal field for the start leg, which discards a weighted local result and reads a shared workspace that the next query overwrites.

Decision

Follow-time smoothing with the exact pixel predicate from the cog's real position.

Rejected: query-time polyline smoothing, because the cog is rarely on the polyline, and a Bresenham walk over cells proves a staircase, not the chord.

Decision

Semantic target identity is (class, source cell), not the bucket.

Rejected: the bucket in the identity, which forces a replan every 48 ticks with an unchanged target.

Decision

The hazard overlay is immutable. The per-bucket safe results live in a separate bounded cache.

Rejected: one object holding both, which contradicts the ownership and lifecycle claims.

Decision

Integer fixed-point costs (128/181) with the exact integer octile heuristic. uint8 next-hop portal fields.

Rejected: float costs, because tie outcomes drift across builds. A Euclidean heuristic in the same units, which is not admissible (181/128 < √2). uint16 distance fields, which take twice the memory, and 1/16 px units overflow on a 436-cell chain. An oracle that shares the quantisation of the search, which hides exactly the error that the 3 % gate exists to catch.

Decision

Zone data: consume damage, gate on zoneDamageByPaint, and pass the live elapsed zone tick into the step.

Rejected: the arrival surface, which gives false safe lanes under wall art. The absolute sim tick, because the schedule runs on elapsed ticks. A snapshot of gameStartTick at FirstLight construction, because it is -1 until startGame, and it changes between games.

Decision

Room "contains dry cells" comes from a per-room maxDamageArrival computed once at overlay build.

Rejected: a linear pass over all nav cells on every bucket roll, which is 343,256 reads every 48 ticks on colossal for a value that never changes.

6 · Gates

  • The gates come from the task. The design must pass them, not redefine them.
  • Legality and reachability are absolute. Cost inflation, body time and activation are numeric thresholds, measured on the canonical linux/amd64 build.
  • Structural gates forbid any full-grid search on a play tick and any map-sized per-seat query state.
GateThresholdWhere and how measured
Legality and reachability Every route is collision-legal and ends at the same validated goal and component as the current planner. A missing route is a failure, never a sample. The route corpus (below). The offline coverage proof of §4.1 for topology.
Cost inflation p95 ≤ 3.0 % and max ≤ 10.0 %, overall and within every stratum, with each stratum reported separately. An empty stratum is a failure. The checked-in literal corpus: 64 pool maps, 16-seat published and 32-seat supported rosters, cpDefault, cpCarrier and cpHunter, four dynamic snapshots (none, literal danger, literal blocked cell, both), near and far. C(R) = the sum over consecutive steps, after deterministic 4 px resampling, of Euclidean step length x (1 + profileDangerWeight x danger(midpoint)), x8 when the midpoint's NavCell is the blocked cell. Inflation = 100 x (C(candidate) - C(current)) / C(current). After cover-hold lands, the corpus gains a cold transition to a cover goal.
Body time Complete FirstLight body slice p95 ≤ 4.0 ms and max ≤ 5.0 ms, for 16 and 32 seats. linux/amd64 Docker release (Nim 2.2.4, pinned nimby.lock, -d:release -d:useMalloc --threads:on --opt:speed), one production-equivalent CPU, five warmups, 30 repetitions. Timed inclusively: every seat body tick, danger rebuild, query, steering and bookkeeping, but not guest runtime or view encoding. Hard cases: simultaneous first goals for all seats, all-seat moving-goal replans, bucket and danger-generation rollover ticks, stuck replans, worst-degree rooms, cold memo.
Activation time Map + index within 2x the current BodyMap baseline on every published pool map, and within 3x on colossal. Same image, same run as the baseline.
Retained shared nav data ≤ 16 MiB on pool maps. ≤ 32 MiB on colossal. The transient build peak is reported separately. Subtask 02's index gate run.
No live full-grid search No active-play tick schedules or advances a full-grid search. Asserted in tests and diagnostics.
Bounded per-seat query state Per-seat query state contains no array sized by map width, height or fine-node count. Asserted at activation.
Versioning GameVersion bumped, nine fixtures re-recorded, viewer rebuilt. After the gates above pass.

7 · Risks and mitigations

  • The latency risks are the cold memo burst and huge rooms. Both have a fixed-cap or sampling fallback that never raises the playing-tick cap.
  • The correctness risks are the static-segment trade, two-sided anchors, and clock or platform drift. Each fails loudly, or a gate bounds it.
  • The hazard model is conservative-late and single-label. Both are accepted with a named adversarial test.
Risk Cold memo burst. All seats query in the same tick with empty memos. Mitigation: the benchmark includes exactly that case. The fallback is to sample every second segment cell (half the reads), or a per-tick memo-fill cap that degrades the remaining edges to static cost deterministically.
Risk Rooms are huge (70k cells). The same-room path relies on the straight line, the via-portal candidate and a local A* with a fixed latency-derived cap, never a cap derived from room size. On a cap hit, the portal candidate's full inflation counts in its stratum, and failing the 10 % max means adding structural alternatives to the index rather than raising the playing-tick cap.
Risk A static segment cannot dodge danger inside a room. This is the trade that the task accepts. Endpoint refinement and the cost gate bound it.
Risk Portal pairs unreachable inside their room (1-5 %). The abstract graph routes through neighbouring rooms. If the gate shows inflation from this, extend the build-time Dijkstra to a bounded margin outside the room.
Risk Two-sided portal anchors that cannot be joined by a bounded crossing search. This is an activation failure with a message naming the choke. Measured mismatch counts are 0-12 per map.
Risk The hazard arrival estimate is conservative-late. This is safe only because the hazard is a price, not a prune. The document and the code carry the warning from PR #408.
Risk Zone clock origin drift, for example a config reload mid-episode. The overlay stores the fingerprint. A mismatch at the safe-cache key is an assertion, not a silent stale answer.
Risk Determinism drift between arm64 development and linux/amd64 canonical builds. Route choice uses integer arithmetic everywhere. The corpus hashes are compared across both platforms.
Risk Search-side danger quantisation (1/256) versus the float oracle. Bounded at 0.49 % relative error on a carrier-profile path. It is reported, and never hidden by quantisation of the oracle.
Risk Single-label search versus a time-dependent hazard. The side-graph A* carries one (cost, physical length) label per node, but a slightly dearer, shorter prefix can reach a later segment before the paint does. Qualification includes that adversarial case. If the single-label search misses the intended safe route, the search keeps an earliest-arrival alternative label per node, never a larger playing-tick cap.

10 · Review record

  • Three Codex rounds: round 1 fact checks and simplifications, round 2 REVISE with seven corrections, round 3 GO with three implementation-proof notes.
  • Everything accepted is in §4 and §5 above. Nothing stays as a dissent.

Round 1: fact checks and simplifications, all accepted

Fact checks included: the damage versus arrival surface distinction. The derated ETA speed. The current connector scan has no bound40, so the new attach needs its own bound. One-based versus zero-based room ids. Colossal has 13,730 pairs and 27,460 directed edges. Per-seat danger rasters are 2.75 MB on colossal. The zone field is built on first use and needs a server seam. There are nine fixtures. The overlay must be separate from the mutable safe cache.

Simplifications accepted: one legality oracle. No room-repair BFS. No portalArrival array. No bucket in target identity. No prefix truncation. Class resolution before the arrived test.

Round 2: REVISE, seven corrections, all accepted

  1. The Euclidean heuristic was not admissible in 1/16 px units. The exact octile heuristic replaced it. Portal fields became uint8 next-hops. The cost oracle stays float.
  2. Portal sides are the graph nodes, and routes store edge IDs with direction, because collapsed portal nodes let a room change skip the crossing cost.
  3. The query assembles and validates the route descriptor in shared scratch before an atomic copy. The segment memo is shared and query-local, not per seat.
  4. The local search cap is a latency-derived constant, never room size.
  5. No gameStartTick snapshot, because it is -1 until startGame. The server passes the elapsed zone tick into the step.
  6. The 8 px coverage becomes an exhaustive offline proof in subtask 02.
  7. Room dry flags come from a per-room maxDamageArrival, and hazard pricing advances the ETA cell by cell.

Round 3: GO, with three implementation-proof notes folded in

  1. Memo accounting: the shared segment-cost memo is counted in the retained shared total (§4.3, item 4).
  2. The octile heuristic is computed on cell-index deltas, with the rounding rule stated in the oracle (§4.3, item 3).
  3. An adversarial earliest-arrival hazard case is part of qualification (§7, last risk).
